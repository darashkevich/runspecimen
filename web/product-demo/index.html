Peace of mind for automated tasks

You approve what may run.
Then you keep the receipt.

When an assistant or a script is about to do something that matters on your Mac, a green “done” is not enough. RunSpecimen lets you read a short plan, type APPROVE yourself, and keep a receipt of what actually ran. Nothing leaves this computer.

What you actually get

Four quiet promises, in plain language. This is not a lock on the rest of your Mac, and it does not prove your work is true.

  • Control

    Nothing in the plan runs until a person types APPROVE at a real keyboard. Assistants may draft the plan; they cannot approve it for you.

  • Trust

    Approval is bound to a snapshot: the command, the source files, and the program that will execute. If any of that changes afterward, the run is refused.

  • Proof

    After the run, you get a receipt of what happened. You can re-check that the results still match — and show someone else the same evidence.

  • Safety

    Approvals expire. Only one run at a time in a workspace. A failed or unfinished step cannot quietly become the foundation for the next one.

    What RunSpecimen refuses

    The product is most useful at the moments people skip paperwork. These refusals are the point.

    • No approval

      It will not run the plan until a human has typed APPROVE. Approval that is piped or unattended is refused.

    • Drift

      If the command, the source files, or the program change after approval, the next step stops. You approve what you reviewed — not a later edit.

    • Stale approval

      Approvals expire. An old “yes” cannot launch a run hours later when the situation may have changed.

    • Altered results

      Postflight and verify re-check declared outputs against the receipt. A changed result file does not pass as the certified run.

    The real sequence, in five beats

    1. 1

      You approve

      A person types APPROVE on a real keyboard. That allows this plan, these files, and this program — with an expiry.

    2. 2

      Get ready

      Before it starts, it checks that your yes is still fresh, nothing changed, the result is not already sitting there, and this folder is not busy.

    3. 3

      It runs

      Exactly the command you allowed, once, within the time limit. One folder, one run at a time.

    4. 4

      Check results

      Compare what happened with what you allowed, then keep a receipt. The next step cannot start until this check exists.

    5. 5

      Receipt

      Re-check the live files against the receipt. If they still match, you have a record of what ran.

    What this is not

    Narrow promises keep the product honest. RunSpecimen records evidence and enforces order. It does not replace the rest of a safety stack.

    • Not an operating-system sandbox. The default setup does not confine the program from the rest of your computer. Pair it with a container or equivalent if you need isolation.
    • Not proof the work is true. A green certificate means the approved contract ran and the declared checks passed — not that the science or the engineering claim is correct.
    • Not a job scheduler. No calendars, no watchers, no fleet of parallel workers in one workspace. One approved, bounded run at a time.
    • Not a remote control plane. It operates on a local workspace. This demo does not approve or execute anything.

    RunSpecimen helps you approve what an automated task may do, then keep a receipt. Assistants draft; you approve; receipts remember.
